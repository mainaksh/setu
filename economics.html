Every transfer is a tiny, profitable unit. The customer pays under 1% — a fraction of incumbents — and we still keep a healthy contribution margin, because a stablecoin rail costs cents to run. Here are the numbers, transparently.

Unit economics of one transfer

A $270 transfer, dollar by dollar.

Take the flagship blue-collar case: a worker sends ~AED 1,000 (≈$270). We charge ~0.9% all-in — far below the ~6.5% a bank takes — and still net a positive margin after paying everyone in the chain.

Net take rate ≈ 0.31% on this low-value transfer. Higher-value (white-collar) transfers carry far more absolute margin for the same effort, pulling the blended net take toward ~0.4–0.6% as the mix matures. Crucially, the per-transfer margin is positive from day one — we don't need scale to make a single transfer profitable; we need scale to cover fixed costs.

Illustrative model. Spreads, partner fees and FX vary by corridor, volume and market conditions. Net take rate range (~0.3–0.6%) is consistent with the scenarios on the Market tab.

The unlock: cheap to acquire, sticky to keep

Remittance is the rare consumer product with built-in retention.

A worker sends money home every month — for years. And every transfer involves family, so the product spreads along relationships instead of expensive ads. That combination is what makes the economics work.

LIFETIME VALUE (net contribution) ~$42

~1.2 transfers/month × ~$0.83 net × a multi-year, high-retention relationship. Higher-value senders are multiples of this.

COST TO ACQUIRE ~$10

Referral credits (both ends are warm contacts) + agent-led distribution keep paid acquisition minimal.

LTV : CAC ≈ 4×+

Illustrative. Assumes high annual retention typical of recurring remittance behaviour; actual figures depend on cohort data once live.

🔁

Recurring by nature

Sending money home isn't a one-off purchase — it's a monthly obligation. Retention is structural, not engineered.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Referral-native

Senders invite receivers; receivers become senders. Acquisition is largely organic and self-funding.

📉

Falling CAC at scale

As density grows in a corridor, word-of-mouth dominates and blended CAC drops further.

Path to profitability

Positive per transfer from day one. Profitable at corridor density.

Because each transfer is already margin-positive, profitability is simply a question of covering a lean fixed-cost base. The honest break-even, with a disciplined team:

CONTRIBUTION BREAK-EVEN AT ROUGHLY ~150k monthly active senders — about 3% of the UAE→India corridor
That's a demanding but very reachable target inside a corridor with millions of senders — and it lines up with the "beachhead won" scenario on the Market tab. Everything above 3% share is operating leverage: fixed costs are largely covered, so incremental transfers drop toward pure contribution.
⚖️

Spreads compress

More liquidity providers competing per corridor tightens spreads — improving the user price and our reliability at once.

🧱

Fixed costs leverage

Compliance, engineering and infra are largely fixed. Each new corridor and user spreads them thinner.
