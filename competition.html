Competition

Everyone improved the old rail.
We changed the rail.

Wise made it transparent. Remitly made it digital. Western Union made it everywhere. All of them, though, still run on correspondent banking and pre-funded float. Setu runs on a stablecoin — a different cost structure entirely. That's the difference between competing and out-positioning.

The landscape

Five kinds of competitor, five different weaknesses.

LEGACY CASH MTOs

Western Union, MoneyGram

Unmatched physical reach — 500k+ agent locations, cash pickup anywhere. The default for the unbanked.

✕ Expensive; FX margin hidden in the rate; built on costly cash handling they can't shed.
DIGITAL MTOs

Remitly, WorldRemit, TapTap Send

App-first, faster, cheaper than legacy. Strong on specific corridors.

✕ Still ride banking rails + pre-funded float; FX markup; corridor-by-corridor cost.
FINTECH / NEOBANK

Wise, Revolut, NRI neobanks

Transparent pricing (Wise from ~0.33%), mid-market rates, great UX for the banked.

✕ Needs a bank account both ends; float in every country caps how low cost can go; can't reach cash users.
BANKS

Traditional retail banks

Trusted, universal, integrated with accounts.

✕ The worst price (~15% avg) and slowest; remittance is an afterthought.
CRYPTO-NATIVE

Stablecoin wallets & apps

Cheapest rail in theory; instant; borderless.

✕ Make the user learn crypto — wallets, seed phrases, volatility. Mass adoption stalls at the UX.
SETU

Stablecoin rail · invisible UX

The cheap rail of crypto-native, the simplicity of a fintech app, and reach into cash via agents — combined.

✓ Cheapest structure + simplest experience + unbanked reach.

Why this is structural, not just execution

We're not on a better point of the old curve. We're on a different curve.

A faster horse is still a horse. The incumbents are all optimizing the correspondent-banking-and-float model. Setu's cost base is a near-free stablecoin transfer plus a thin local spread — a structurally lower floor that no amount of incumbent optimization reaches.

🧱

No correspondent chain

One stablecoin hop replaces a multi-bank relay. The biggest cost and delay simply disappears.

💧

No pre-funded float everywhere

Incumbents tie up capital in every country. Setu's LP network supplies liquidity on demand, freeing that capital.

⚡

Instant finality

Settlement in seconds, not days — a product difference the customer feels every single time.

The question every investor asks

"But isn't Wise already cheap and great?"

Yes — and we respect it. But three things keep the door open. First, Wise still needs a bank account on both ends, which excludes the cash-based, unbanked workers who are the heart of the Gulf→South Asia corridor. Second, its float-in-every-country model has a cost floor a stablecoin rail sits beneath. Third, it's a broad, global product — not a corridor-deep one obsessed with the UAE→India lane, the local agents, the merchant loop, and the languages of that specific population. We win by going narrow and deep where a global player goes wide.

And critically: the customer Setu serves first — the cash-paid worker without an easy bank account — is one Wise structurally can't serve at all.
The moat

Why this gets harder to copy over time, not easier.

💧

Liquidity density

Each LP and agent we add tightens spreads, which wins more flow, which attracts more LPs. A latecomer faces a network that's already cheaper.

⚖️

Regulatory licenses

The licensing and partner relationships built corridor-by-corridor are slow, expensive, and hard to replicate — a real barrier.

🎭

Invisible-UX craft

Hiding crypto well is deceptively hard. The execution that makes it feel like a money app is a compounding product advantage.

🪙

Token-aligned network

SETU incentives bind liquidity providers to the network's success, creating loyalty a clone can't simply buy.

🤝

Corridor trust

In remittance, trust is everything. Becoming the default in one diaspora community is a brand moat that travels by word of mouth.

🏪

The merchant loop

Once workers earn and spend within Setu, switching means leaving an entire local economy — not just an app.
