Tokenomics

SETU is the network's
coordination layer.

It is not a stablecoin, and it is not a get-rich pitch aimed at the people sending money home. SETU exists to coordinate, secure, and reward the liquidity providers and agents who make the rail run — its value tracks real network usage, not hype.

First, the honesty

What SETU is — and deliberately is not.

The cleanest tokenomics start by being clear about what the token is for. This separation is both good design and good compliance.

✓ SETU is

  • ✓ A utility token: staked by liquidity providers for the right to serve flow
  • ✓ A governance token: holders vote on network parameters and treasury
  • ✓ An incentive: rewards LPs, agents and ecosystem contributors
  • ✓ Value-linked to real usage — more flow needs more staked liquidity

✕ SETU is not

  • ✕ A stablecoin — value moves over regulated USDC, not SETU
  • ✕ Something a sender ever needs to buy, hold, or understand
  • ✕ Marketed to retail as an investment or a promise of returns
  • ✕ A substitute for revenue — the business earns fees regardless
A grandmother sending ₹22,640 home will never see the word "SETU." The token lives entirely in the operator layer — which is exactly why adoption isn't gated on anyone speculating.
The four jobs SETU does

Every token has to earn its place. Here's how SETU earns its.

💧

1 · Stake for liquidity rights

Liquidity providers stake SETU to gain the right to fill payouts and earn transaction fees. More network flow demands more staked liquidity — creating organic, usage-driven demand for the token.

🏷️

2 · Fee rebates & alignment

Operators and high-volume partners holding SETU receive fee rebates, aligning the people who run the rail with the network's long-term health.

🗳️

3 · Governance

Holders vote on corridor parameters, fee schedules, treasury allocation, and which markets to open next — progressively decentralizing control.

🎁

4 · Incentives & rewards

Agents, early liquidity, and ecosystem builders are rewarded in SETU, bootstrapping the network effects that make the rail cheap and reliable.

Value accrual

Token value should track usage. Here's the loop that makes it.

As real transfer volume grows, more local-currency liquidity is needed; serving that flow requires staking SETU; and a share of network fees can be directed to a buyback-and-distribute or burn mechanism — tying token demand to the actual business, not narrative.

THE FLYWHEEL Usage → staking demand → tighter spreads → more usage
The point: token demand is anchored to the volume of real money moving through Setu. If the business works, the token has a reason to exist; if it doesn't, no amount of tokenomics would have saved it. Revenue first, token second.

Specific value-accrual mechanisms (staking yields, buyback/burn, fee-share) are draft and subject to legal, tax and regulatory review before any implementation.

Allocation

Fixed supply, weighted toward building the network.

  • 25% · Ecosystem & growth (250M). Corridor launches, merchant onboarding, referrals, partnerships.

  • 8% · Community & public (80M). Fair, capped distribution where legally permitted.

Illustrative draft allocation. Final supply, splits and mechanics are subject to legal/regulatory review and may differ. Token may not be offered or sold in certain jurisdictions or to certain persons.

Vesting & emissions

No cliffs to dump on. Incentives that unlock slowly.

A credible token has long lockups for insiders and gradual emissions for rewards — so supply enters in step with real network growth, not in a flood.

Why this design is the safe one

Utility-first, retail-protected, revenue-backed.

By keeping value movement on a regulated stablecoin and confining SETU to the operator layer, the design avoids the two traps that sink token projects: dependence on speculation, and selling a token that behaves like an unregistered security to retail users. The business stands on fee revenue; the token coordinates the network on top of it.

Token structures carry real legal risk and vary sharply by jurisdiction. Everything here is a draft concept and not an offer, solicitation, or promise. A token would only ever be issued after thorough legal, tax and securities review, with appropriate jurisdictional restrictions — and possibly not at all if the business is better served without one.
